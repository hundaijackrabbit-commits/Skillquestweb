# Communication is a coordination system

Sending more messages does not create more understanding. In a busy workplace, communication succeeds when the right people share an interpretation of the outcome, constraints, ownership, and next action.

The problem has become easier to see because work leaves a digital trail. In its [2025 analysis of the “infinite workday”](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/breaking-down-infinite-workday), Microsoft reported that the highest-volume 20% of users in its dataset received an average of 275 pings over a full day, with an average interval of two minutes during an eight-hour core workday. The same report found that 60% of meetings in the measured high-meeting group were unscheduled or ad hoc.

Those figures do not describe every worker. They do illustrate a design challenge: communication can consume the attention required to do the work it is meant to coordinate.

## Five moves that improve the signal

### 1. Lead with the outcome

The first useful sentence should tell the reader why the message exists. State the decision, request, or material update before providing history.

Instead of *“I wanted to follow up on the document we discussed and mention a few timing concerns,”* write: *“Please approve moving the review to Thursday; two required test results will not be available by Tuesday.”*

The second version gives the reader a decision and the reason it matters.

### 2. Design the handoff

A handoff should make five things visible:

- the output being transferred;
- its current status;
- the receiving owner;
- the acceptance standard;
- the next deadline or escalation point.

This matters even more when an AI tool participates. A prompt may start a task, but the surrounding communication still needs to specify approved sources, review requirements, and who owns the final result.

### 3. Match detail to the decision

Different readers require different evidence. A specialist may need reproduction steps and edge cases. A sponsor may need impact, options, risk, and a recommendation.

Audience awareness is not hiding information. It is arranging the information so the reader can perform their role. Link to deeper evidence rather than forcing every detail into the main message.

### 4. Listen for the model underneath the words

[Active listening](/skills/active-listening) goes beyond waiting for a turn to speak. Identify what the other person is trying to achieve, what constraint shapes their view, and which assumption may differ from yours.

A useful check sounds like this: “I hear that protecting the launch date matters more than keeping every feature. Is that the trade-off you want us to use?”

The response can correct a misunderstanding before it becomes rework.

### 5. Close the loop

Consequential communication should end with an observable confirmation. Depending on the situation, that may be a named owner and due date, a decision record, a read-back, or a test of the result.

“Let me know if you have questions” places the burden on the reader. “Maya will revise the brief by noon; Theo will verify the numbers before client review” establishes a shared plan.

## Choose the right channel

Use a simple decision rule:

- **Record:** If people will need the information later, write it down.
- **Discuss:** If ambiguity or emotion is high, use a conversation.
- **Decide:** If a choice affects scope, risk, money, or ownership, capture the decision afterward.
- **Escalate:** If waiting increases the cost of failure, name the issue and deadline directly.

A meeting should not be the default container for every unclear message. A long written thread should not substitute for a sensitive conversation.

## A one-week communication experiment

Choose one recurring workflow and record three measures for a week:

1. How many clarification messages were needed?
2. How often did ownership or timing have to be restated?
3. How much work was redone because the acceptance standard was unclear?

Then introduce one change: a structured request, a written decision note, or an owner–action–deadline close. Compare the next week with the baseline.

This creates better evidence than simply feeling more organized.

## How to demonstrate the skill

Employers cannot observe “excellent communicator” on a résumé. They can inspect artifacts and outcomes. Useful proof includes a concise decision memo, a meeting system that reduced follow-ups, a difficult stakeholder conversation with a clear resolution, or instructions that another person could execute accurately.

Practise [professional communication](/skills/communication) alongside [project management](/skills/project-management). The first improves shared meaning; the second ensures that meaning becomes controlled action.
